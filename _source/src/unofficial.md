# A Little More About Me ♡
*For the curious souls who wandered beyond my résumé.*

Hello again, stranger! ♡

If you've made it this far, I suppose you'd like to know a little more about the girl behind all the projects, research papers, and rather serious-looking academic achievements.

Well, where do I begin?

I'm a girl with a rather hopeless affection for things that don't exist. Magical kingdoms, impossible technologies, fictional characters who have absolutely no business making me cry, and worlds so beautifully imagined that returning to reality feels a little unfair.

I love stories. I love art. I love the way a beautiful piece of music can make an ordinary afternoon feel like a scene from a movie.

And somewhere between all these little loves, I fell rather unexpectedly in love with computer science.

Funny how life works, isn't it?

## Of Fictional Worlds and Other Great Loves

I have always believed that imagination is one of humanity's loveliest gifts.

Think about it. Someone, somewhere, can dream up an entire universe, give it a history, fill it with people who have never existed, and somehow make millions of strangers feel as though they've lived there.

How extraordinary is that?

I'm an enthusiastic consumer of fantasy and fiction in almost every form. Novels, manga, manhwa, manhua, anime, movies... if there's a world worth getting lost in, chances are I'll happily disappear into it.

One of my dearest favorites is **Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint**.

There's something incredibly romantic about the idea of a reader who knows a story so intimately that, when the world of that story becomes reality, his love and knowledge of it become his greatest strengths.

Perhaps that's what I adore most about Kim Dokja. He isn't simply someone who reads stories. He's someone whose life was shaped by one. And when the time comes, he gets to become part of it.

Isn't that every reader's secret little dream?

Then there's **How to Live as a Villain**, which appeals to a slightly less innocent side of my literary tastes. Hehe.

Not every protagonist needs to be a shining knight with an impeccable moral compass. Sometimes, I enjoy watching a clever, selfish, wonderfully questionable character scheme his way through a world that isn't particularly kind itself.

Would I endorse all his decisions? Absolutely not.

Would I eagerly turn the page to see what outrageous thing he does next?

...Unfortunately, yes. ♡

## A Heart Full of Anime

If you ask me about my favorite anime, **Attack on Titan** will probably escape my lips before you've even finished the question.

I adore it.

Not merely for its battles or breathtaking storytelling, but for the uncomfortable questions it leaves behind.

What is freedom worth? How far should someone go to protect the people they love? Can two people stand on opposite sides of a war and both believe, with all their hearts, that they're doing the right thing?

I think what makes Attack on Titan so unforgettable is that its characters are painfully human. They're brave and frightened, loving and cruel, selfish and selfless, sometimes all at once.

I recognize little fragments of humanity in them. Perhaps even fragments of myself.

And then, when my heart has endured quite enough tragedy, there's **Howl's Moving Castle**.

Oh, Howl's Moving Castle. ♡

The rolling green hills, the little towns tucked beneath enormous skies, the music, the magic, the strange moving castle, the quiet tenderness of it all.

It feels like a world painted with love.

Some stories make you want to know what happens next. Others make you wish you could step through the screen, settle into a little cottage, and stay there forever.

Howl's Moving Castle is the latter for me.

I think I could spend an entire lifetime in a world like that, growing flowers, watching sunsets, and occasionally falling in love with a mysterious wizard who has a flair for dramatics.

Though, knowing myself, I'd probably end up trying to figure out how the castle works.

## How I Fell in Love with Computer Science

I don't think my love for computer science began with a programming language.

It began with a question.

**What if the impossible wasn't quite so impossible after all?**

I grew up fascinated by stories like *The Matrix* and *Sword Art Online*, where the boundaries between reality and simulation become wonderfully, terrifyingly blurred.

Imagine stepping into a game and feeling the warmth of the sunlight against your skin. Imagine touching the grass, tasting food, making friends, falling in love, and living an entire second life in a world built from nothing but human imagination and technology.

Not watching it.

Not controlling a character on a screen.

Actually *being there*.

I remember watching the Alicization arc of Sword Art Online and becoming utterly fascinated by the Underworld, a simulated reality inhabited by artificial beings who grew up, formed relationships, learned, loved, and understood themselves to be human.

And then there was Kirito, whose injured mind was treated through an experimental technology that allowed his consciousness to experience this extraordinary world.

I couldn't stop thinking about it.

What if one day we could create something even remotely similar?

What if such technology could help patients undergoing neurological rehabilitation? What if scientists could explore complex experiments in extraordinarily realistic simulated environments? What if distance, disability, or the limitations of our physical world no longer determined the boundaries of what someone could experience?

I know, I know. It sounds terribly ambitious.

But isn't that how some of the most beautiful inventions begin?

With someone looking at an impossible thing and quietly wondering, *"But what if?"*

Somewhere along the way, I realized that computer science was more than a discipline for building software.

It was a way to give imagination a fighting chance at becoming reality.

And I wanted to be part of that.

## The Curious Little Researcher

As much as I adore imaginary worlds, there's something equally enchanting about discovering how our real one works.

I love learning.

Not just the comfortable kind, where someone explains something beautifully and everything makes sense, but also the frustrating kind. The kind where you spend days wrestling with an idea, question everything you thought you understood, and then, quite suddenly, something clicks.

That little moment of discovery?

Oh, I live for those.

My journey into research has been one of the loveliest surprises of my academic life.

Over the past year, I've had the opportunity to work closely with my professor on four research projects, two of which have developed into research papers.

My own research has explored something that might sound a little less magical at first: predicting how much energy neural networks consume during training.

But to me, there's a beautiful question hidden beneath all those numbers.

**Can we teach our increasingly intelligent machines to be a little kinder to the world that sustains them?**

Artificial intelligence is extraordinary. Yet the computational infrastructure behind it demands energy, materials, and resources.

I find myself dreaming about a future where intelligence doesn't have to come at such a tremendous environmental cost.

Smaller, more efficient models. Smarter computing systems. Data centers that work in greater harmony with the environment.

Perhaps even technologies we haven't imagined yet.

And now, I hope to begin a PhD, to spend a few wonderful years exploring questions that don't have answers yet.

I know research isn't always glamorous. There will be failed experiments, stubborn problems, papers that refuse to cooperate, and days when I wonder why I voluntarily signed up for all this.

But I think there's something profoundly romantic about dedicating years of your life to understanding a tiny piece of the universe a little better.

Especially when that understanding might someday help someone else.

## A Love Letter to Tomorrow

Sometimes I wonder what the world will look like a hundred years from now.

Will our cities be filled with gardens and sunlight? Will our buildings breathe with greenery, our machines run quietly on cleaner energy, and our technologies exist in harmony with nature?

Will we have created artificial intelligence that expands human creativity rather than replacing our desire to think?

Or will we become so accustomed to machines knowing everything that we gradually forget how to understand things ourselves?

I don't know.

Perhaps that's what makes the future so fascinating.

I find myself drawn to the idea of a *solarpunk* world. One where technological advancement and the natural world aren't enemies, but partners in something beautiful.

A world where progress isn't measured only by how much we can build, but by how thoughtfully we build it.

I don't believe technology is inherently our salvation or our downfall. I think it is, in many ways, a reflection of the people creating it: our ambitions, our fears, our generosity, and sometimes our carelessness.

And that gives me hope.

Because if the future is something we create, then surely we can choose to create something worth loving.

I don't expect to change the world single-handedly. I'm only one curious girl with a laptop, a head full of ideas, and an admittedly unreasonable number of dreams.

But perhaps I can contribute a little.

A question answered. A system improved. An idea shared. A discovery that becomes the foundation for something wonderful long after I'm gone.

I think that would be a lovely way to spend a lifetime.

## The Girl Behind the Screen

Of course, there is more to me than research, technology, and fictional people who occasionally ruin my emotional stability.

I have always loved drawing.

There's something peaceful about watching a blank page slowly become something that existed only in your imagination moments before. I think art and programming are rather similar in that way. Both begin with an idea, a little patience, and the stubborn belief that you can make something out of nothing.

I enjoy staying active, going to the gym, and discovering what my body is capable of. I love the thought of swimming, traveling, exploring unfamiliar places, and collecting little memories from corners of the world I've never seen.

I'm also quite fond of beautiful things. Not necessarily expensive things, mind you. Sometimes it's a lovely landscape, an unexpectedly pretty sky, a melody, an illustration, or a sentence in a book that makes me stop reading just to admire it.

I can be ambitious, occasionally impatient, and perhaps a little too emotionally invested in the futures I've imagined for myself.

I dream big. Sometimes embarrassingly big.

And when things don't unfold the way I hoped, I feel that deeply too.

But I suppose that's part of being someone who loves things wholeheartedly.

When I was little, my father used to tell me something I've carried with me ever since:

**"Everything worth doing is worth doing well."**

I don't think I understood the weight of those words back then. They were simply something my father would say, one of those little pieces of wisdom that quietly settle into your childhood and make a home somewhere in your heart.

Years passed, and somewhere along my academic journey, I heard those very words again.

This time, from my professor.

I remember how strangely wonderful it felt. As though, for just a moment, the little girl I used to be and the woman I was becoming had met somewhere in the middle.

And suddenly, those familiar words meant something new.

Perhaps doing something well isn't always about being the best, or getting everything right. Perhaps it's about giving a little piece of yourself to the things you believe are worth doing. About caring enough to try, even when no one is watching.

I think that's the kind of person I'd like to keep becoming.

Someone who works hard, loves deeply, remains curious, and never becomes too grown-up to dream.

And if, somewhere along the way, I get to leave behind something beautiful, something that makes another person's world a little better...

Well, wouldn't that be a lovely story to have lived? ♡

*With love,*

*Just a girl, somewhere between reality and a daydream.* ♡
