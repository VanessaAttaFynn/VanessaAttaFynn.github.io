Leadership

Leading by building the thing myself.

In 2026 I was elected Chair of the Abstract Review Committee for my university's DCS Postgraduate Research Conference — and ended up building the entire system the committee ran on.

Scroll
01 The role

Elected Chairperson

I ran for and won the Chairperson election for the Abstract Review Committee of the GRCW/DCS 2026 postgraduate research conference, with Prince Amofa elected Vice Chairperson alongside me.

The committee was responsible for reviewing every abstract submitted to the conference across multiple thematic research tracks — from Machine Learning and Data Science to HCI, Networks, and Cybersecurity — before the conference could go ahead.

02 The framework

Designing how 281 abstracts get reviewed fairly

Before any review could happen, I designed the process itself: a multi-stage review framework (Stages 0–4) with fan-out/fan-in assignment logic, so abstracts could be distributed across reviewers by track and then reconciled back into final decisions.

I wrote this up formally as a Plan of Action, which went through review by my supervising lecturer before the committee started work — and I ran the committee's first meeting to walk everyone through it.

03 The build

When nothing fit, I built it

Partway into planning, it became clear that no existing tool matched the review workflow the committee actually needed. So I built UGAbstracts — a full Yii2/SQL Server workflow engine with rubric scoring, role-based access, and automatic conflict-of-interest detection — and deployed it myself.

I chaired the committee, wrote the process it followed, and built the software it ran on, all while personally reviewing 15 abstracts as Chair — alongside a full-time job and an MSc thesis running in parallel.

04 Reflection

What it actually took

Chairing a committee is a governance job — framework, fairness, meetings, decisions. Building the platform underneath it is an engineering job. Doing both at once meant every gap in the process became a bug I had to fix myself, and every technical constraint became something I had to explain and defend to the committee. That loop — between designing a process and building the tool that enforces it — is the part of this I'd sign up for again.
