Leadership

Keeping a conference on schedule.

In 2026 I was elected Chair of the Abstract Review Committee for my department's Postgraduate Research Conference & Workshop — leading 17 postgraduate reviewers through 284 submissions, from the first plan to the final Book of Abstracts. During my MSc I was also course representative for two courses.

Scroll
01 The role

Elected Chair

I was elected Chair of a 17-member Abstract Review Committee responsible for the conference's entire abstract pipeline: submission, peer review, selection, and the Book of Abstracts.

Before any review began, I wrote the committee's Plan of Action and Terms of Reference and got them approved. As the work moved forward, I reported our progress to the conference's Advisory Committee and updated the plan on the feedback we received.

02 Leading the team

Seventeen reviewers, one standard

I onboarded the committee, ran our weekly meetings, and walked everyone through how the review would work. Together we agreed the rubrics, the pass marks and the tools we would use, so every abstract was judged the same way.

I organised and oversaw the double-blind peer review sessions — a two-stage pipeline of initial screening (plagiarism, theme and file checks) followed by an academic quality review — and kept the team moving until every review was in.

03 When things went wrong

Pressure, handled

The submission portal opened late, so the deadline moved back two weeks — and about 80% of the 284 abstracts arrived on or around the new deadline, leaving only days to review them all.

Midway through review, the plagiarism checker went down. I switched the team to a backup tool so screening could continue without losing time. I then coordinated with the Programmes Committee to allocate 150 oral and 134 poster slots, and delivered the Book of Abstracts before the conference opened.

04 The tools

Building what the team needed

No existing tool fit the workflow we had agreed, so I built UGAbstracts, the review system the committee worked in, and a live scoring module that lecturers and panelists used to score presentations during the conference.

05 Course representative

The link between lecturers and the class

During my MSc I was course representative for Advanced Data Visualization, taught by Prof. Kofi Sarpong Adu-Manu, and Advanced Computer Vision, taught by Prof. Ebenezer Owusu, Head of Department.

I kept information flowing between lecturers and students, sent reminders, scheduled classes and exam timetables, and organised and oversaw peer review sessions.

06 Reflection

What it actually took

Leading fellow students meant there was no authority to lean on — only preparation and follow-through. I tried to stay a step ahead: plans written before they were needed, decisions made together in the room, and problems solved before they became the team's problem. Most of what leadership looked like was invisible: the reminders, the rescheduling, and the quiet fixes that kept everyone else moving.
