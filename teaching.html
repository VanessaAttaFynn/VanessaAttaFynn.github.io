Teaching

Turning technical skills into other people's skills.

Alongside development work, I've taught four students so far — across two very different groups, with very different goals.

01 Overview

Two groups, two goals

01

Young adults (11–20)

Web development fundamentals — HTML, CSS, JavaScript, and Python — for students building their first real technical skills.

02

Professionals (30–45)

Professional-level Microsoft Office proficiency — Word, PowerPoint, and Excel — for working adults adding new skills mid-career.

02 Case study

A six-week intensive, built from scratch

My most recent engagement is a six-week course for an 18-year-old student, covering both the Microsoft Office suite and Python, at four sessions a week.

Rather than teach from generic material, I built a custom slide-deck design system for the course — a consistent dark navy and orange visual identity, generated programmatically with pptxgenjs so every session's materials look professionally consistent rather than assembled ad hoc.

03 Approach

Why this, alongside everything else

Teaching forces a different discipline than building systems does: you can't ship something a student doesn't understand and call it done. It's the same instinct that shows up in the contextual help systems I've built at work — the belief that good documentation and good teaching are really the same skill, aimed at different audiences.
