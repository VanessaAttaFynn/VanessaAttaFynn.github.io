02 Case study

A six-week intensive, built from scratch

My most recent engagement is a six-week course taken by four students aged roughly 12 to 18, covering both the Microsoft Office suite and Python, at three to four sessions a week.

On top of teaching the course itself — with regular assignments, weekly project work and weekend practice sessions, usually one to two hours on Saturdays — I mentored each of them one-on-one, with custom slides and supplementary material. I walked them through installing their tools and finding their way around code editors and IDEs, then taught them to run and debug code on their own.

I also taught them how to research the problems they hit — on Stack Overflow and similar forums — instead of leaning heavily on AI assistants while they're still beginners.

03 Approach

Why this, alongside everything else

Teaching forces a different discipline than building systems does: you can't ship something a student doesn't understand and call it done. It's the same instinct that shows up in the contextual help systems I've built at work — the belief that good documentation and good teaching are really the same skill, aimed at different audiences. I'm not tied to one age group or one subject — if I know it, I'll teach it.
